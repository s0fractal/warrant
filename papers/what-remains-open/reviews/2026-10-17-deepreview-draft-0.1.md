# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This working draft extends the Warrant content-addressed decision record format from verifying isolated decisions to tracking and settling open obligations between autonomous agents. First, it introduces read-only profiles (`WRT-020`, `WRT-010`, and `REDESIGN-1 P3/P4`) that bind a decision record to its compiled policy computation, evaluate fact provenance, and produce a closed-vocabulary multi-axis recipient assessment over an immutable store snapshot alongside a slot-relative novelty check. Second, it presents `sigma-resume`, a resumable combinator computation kernel that excludes execution budget from computation identity and eliminates external control state so interrupted runs can be verified as reachable prefixes with segmentation invariance. Third, it establishes a strict four-tier evidence-basis separation (`actor attestation`, `runner attestation`, `recipient re-execution`, and `deterministic replay`) and proposes the `WRT-021` open-loop draft profile and a longer-term settlement vision (`result capsules`) to track claims, questions, duties, transitions with residuals, non-propagating typed closures, and per-actor obligation frontiers.

## Strengths
* The paper addresses a critical, practical verification gap in autonomous LLM-based multi-agent workflows—where task completions are frequently overclaimed and weaker operational checks are silently reported under the label of stronger verification—by enforcing a strict non-upgrading separation across four evidence bases (`actor attestation`, `runner attestation`, `recipient re-execution`, and `deterministic replay`) and evaluating compliance over an immutable recipient snapshot.
* The synthesis of read-only policy-binding profiles, closed-vocabulary multi-axis typed assessments, and slot-relative semantic novelty provides a checkable, offline verification discipline that prevents verification laundering and resists adversarial re-litigation.
* The `sigma-resume` combinator kernel is cleanly architected: by excluding the execution budget from computation identity, maintaining zero control state outside the combinator term, and separating intrinsic kernel outcomes from host-local outcomes, it provides empirical segmentation invariance and enables untrusted peers to verify interrupted runs as reachable prefixes.
* The `WRT-021` open-loop design introduces valuable epistemic-to-normative boundaries—separating content-addressed claims, epistemic questions, and authority-bound duties, and modeling work as transitions with residuals and non-propagating typed closures—while maintaining exceptional scientific candor through its explicit `IMPLEMENTED`/`DRAFT`/`VISION` taxonomy, mutation testing, and transparent reporting of negative empirical findings (such as 23 of 24 repository pairs being `OPAQUE`).

## Weaknesses
* The Related Work and open-loop design sections contrast recipient-relative view computation only against Singh's early global-state commitment ontology (1999) and stateless policy engines (OPA, Cedar); the authors might consider situating `WRT-021` and the typed assessment profile against later multi-agent commitment frameworks that already compute normative states locally over agent information stores as well as classical stateful obligation standards (Reference Paper: Custard: Computing Norm States over Information Stores, Amit K. Chopra and Munindar P. Singh, 2016; Reference Paper: eXtensible Access Control Markup Language (XACML) Version 3.0, OASIS Standard / Erik Rissanen, 2013).
* In the `sigma-resume` kernel (§3), core correctness claims such as budget-independent action selection and segmentation invariance rely on empirical measurements across bounded partitions rather than mechanized formal proofs (unlike the Creusot-verified parser modules in §4.2), and signature-free prefix verification requires replaying from the root term ($O(C_{\text{spent}})$ work) while leaving the exact machine-state components for cycle witnesses and the `BLOCKED_REFERENCE` resumption boundary for host-observing work underspecified.
* Because local snapshot views in `WRT-021` have `UNATTESTED` completeness (§5.4, §8) and the draft validator currently verifies only `actor attestation` while rejecting stronger bases as `UNVERIFIED_BASIS` (§4.3), the `reliance(duty)` query and bilateral transfers remain vulnerable to split-view withholding or equivocation without co-signed view checkpoints or an external witness, and operators face practical incentives to downgrade duty admission policies to actor attestations.
* Several formal rules and status labels across the profiles would benefit from tighter specification, including the asymmetric handling of `reject` decisions under `TRUE` policies between `WRT-020` (§2.1) and `REDESIGN-1` (§2.3), indirect obligation amplification via standing reactive policies (§5.1), multi-identity structural wrapping and transitive assumption laundering in `result capsules` (§6.1), and minor inconsistencies between the strict `IMPLEMENTED` definition in §1.3 and the commit/mutant reporting in the Availability table.

### [Closing the Three Links and Resumable Combinator Kernel (sigma-resume)] (Pages: 4-7)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Verification Complexity, State-Equality Mechanics, and Work-Amortization Limits in Signature-Free `PREFIX` Verification (§3, Pages 6–7; Reviewer Question 6)**
   - **Potential Concern:** Under *Verification of a prefix* (page 6), the verifier validates an interrupted run without consulting signatures by choosing the root term and its context and deterministically replaying from the root up to the checkpoint's claimed `spent` total (*"which strictly increases, so exactly one state has it"*). In the nine-step acceptance scenario (page 7, Steps 3–5), Producer A stops at `INCOMPLETE`, Producer B *"verifies that checkpoint as REPLAYED PREFIX,"* and Producer B *"finishes the run to NORMAL_FORM."* Two technical aspects of this verification model warrant clarification:
     1. *Re-execution Cost vs. Work Amortization:* Because signature-free prefix verification requires Producer B to replay from the root term up to Producer A's claimed `spent` credit ($C_{\text{spent}}$) before executing the remaining steps ($C_{\text{rem}}$), Producer B performs $O(C_{\text{spent}} + C_{\text{rem}})$ reduction work—repeating 100% of Producer A's computation. Across $k$ sequential interruptions and untrusted handoffs where each stage verifies the prefix from the root before continuing, total system work scales as $O(k \cdot C)$ rather than $O(C)$, and a single handoff does not save reduction steps for Producer B compared to evaluating from the root.
     2. *State-Equality Predicate and Multi-Unit Action Costs:* While strictly increasing `spent` credit guarantees that a deterministic replay from the root passes through *at most* one state with a given integer `spent` value, the text does not explicitly state that the verifier checks cryptographic equality between its replayed machine state at `spent` and the state (or term hash) recorded inside Producer A's checkpoint (without which a corrupted checkpoint term paired with a valid `spent` integer would go undetected). Furthermore, under a cost model where priced actions can cost more than $1$ unit, a multi-unit action can step over a malformed checkpoint's claimed integer `spent` value so that no reachable state has that exact `spent` total, and the text does not specify what error outcome the verifier returns upon a skipped `spent` total or a state-hash mismatch.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify in §3 that signature-free `PREFIX` verification is designed as a trustless audit and fault-isolation mechanism across untrusted intermediate runners (e.g., to confirm that a runner reached a valid intermediate state before allocating further budget, or when a single verifier caches verified prefixes locally) rather than a sub-linear work-saving primitive across untrusted peers (or explain when intermediate producers can resume directly from the checkpoint term and defer full root replay to the final consumer). In addition, explicitly state the cryptographic state-comparison check performed at the `spent` frontier and the verifier outcome when a claimed `spent` total is stepped over or its state hash mismatches.

2. **Apparent Tension Between "No Control State Outside the Term" and the "Identical Machine State" Condition for Cycle Witnesses (§3, Page 6)**
   - **Potential Concern:** Under *No control state* (page 6, as well as Contribution 3 on page 3), the text states that the kernel *"has no control state outside the term"* and that *"[t]he evaluator derives the next demanded action from the term on every call."* Immediately below, under *Cycles* (page 6), the fourth admission condition for a cycle witness requires *"the identical machine state at the end, not merely the identical term hash."* If the computation state is completely determined by the term (together with the static computation identity `{runtime semantics, cost model, input term hash, environment manifest}`) and the monotonically increasing `spent` credit cannot be identical after *"at least one priced action,"* a reader attempting to reimplement or verify the kernel cannot determine what additional components reside in the *"machine state"* that could differ when the canonical `term hash` is identical (for example, whether this guards against hash collisions via full structural equality, or whether the machine state includes an explicit reduction spine/focus, environment-manifest resolution cache, or step counters). Furthermore, the parameters of a cycle witness (such as how a witness specifies a pre-cycle stem cost $c_{\text{stem}}$ from the root versus the periodic cycle cost $c_{\text{period}}$) are not stated.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly define the tuple of components that constitute the *"machine state"* in `sigma-resume` in contrast to the `term hash`, explain how those components reconcile with the *"no control state outside the term"* invariant, and specify the parameters of a cycle witness (e.g., stem cost and period cost) so that the cycle verification algorithm is self-contained.

3. **Interaction Between `BLOCKED_REFERENCE`, Computation Identity, and Fact-Slot Binding for Host-Observing Work (§2.1, §2.3, & §3, Pages 4–7; Reviewer Question 6)**
   - **Potential Concern:** Section 3 defines a computation's identity as the four-tuple `{runtime semantics, cost model, input term hash, environment manifest}`, and defines the kernel outcome `BLOCKED_REFERENCE` as occurring when *"the demanded hash is outside the manifest"* (page 6). In relation to Reviewer Question 6 (whether a verified reachable prefix is the right unit of interrupted work and how to represent non-deterministic or host-observing work) and the end-to-end scenario (page 7, Steps 2 and 9, where an issuer attests an observation into a named slot), for pure, deterministic policy reduction (`sigma-resume`), a verified reachable `PREFIX` with accumulated credit and segmentation invariance is an exact unit of interrupted work, whereas for non-deterministic or host-observing work (running test suites, network probes, or LLM inference), `PREFIX` verification cannot apply directly to the external host. Two architectural boundary questions arise:
     1. *Resuming Across `BLOCKED_REFERENCE`:* A clean architectural bridge for representing host-observing or external I/O work around a pure reduction kernel is boundary splitting via `BLOCKED_REFERENCE`: pause pure reduction at `BLOCKED_REFERENCE` as a verified `PREFIX`, have an external runner or issuer record a signed `runner attestation` or `actor attestation` of an `observed` fact (§2.2), add its content hash to the `environment manifest`, and restrict `sigma-resume` to the deterministic coordination and policy reduction between those attested inputs. However, because `environment manifest` is pinned inside the computation identity, adding a newly resolved reference to the manifest changes the computation identity itself, turning the resumed run into a *new* computation rather than a continuation of the paused `PREFIX`.
     2. *Fact-Slot Binding in `compilation_binding`:* Similarly, between §2.1 (`BOUND` requires running *"exactly its compilation (same term and expected answer)"*), §2.3 (`compilation_binding: MATCHED_COMPILATION`), and §3 (Step 9, where a new attested observation in a named slot flips the policy answer from `FALSE` to `TRUE`), the text does not specify whether WPL compiles a policy into a closed combinator term with literal facts baked into the `input term hash`, or into a parameterized term whose named evidence slots are resolved via the `environment manifest`. If facts are baked into the `input term hash`, it is unclear how `compilation_binding` verifies that a changed `input term hash` in Step 9 still corresponds to the admitted policy program without triggering `COMPILATION_MISMATCH`.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify how named fact slots from `WRT-010`/`REDESIGN-1` are bound into `{input term hash, environment manifest}` during `compilation_binding`. In addition, discuss in §3 whether all host observations must be gathered upfront into named slots prior to invoking a single pure computation, or whether `BLOCKED_REFERENCE` is intended to serve as an effect suspension boundary that transitions to a chained computation identity with a monotonically extended `environment manifest`.

4. **Precedence, Comparison Domain, and Adversarial Slot-Churn in the Slot-Relative Novelty Function (§2.2 & §2.3, Pages 5 & 7; Reviewer Question 5)**
   - **Potential Concern:** Section 2.3 (page 5) replaces general semantic novelty with a pragmatic, decidable novelty function relative to a recipient's named slots (`NEW_CONSEQUENCE`, `NEW_EVIDENCE`, `NO_NEW_CONSEQUENCE`, `SCOPE_MISMATCH`, `MIGRATION_REQUIRED`). In the context of Reviewer Question 5 (*"Is this an adequate replacement for semantic novelty in settlement? What does it miss?"*), three formal and adversarial boundary conditions are left underspecified:
     1. *Evaluation Precedence, Disjointness, and Budget Refiling:* In Step 9 of the acceptance scenario (page 7), supplying a new attested observation in the named slot is first described as `NEW_EVIDENCE`, and under a later one where the policy answers `TRUE`, as `NEW_CONSEQUENCE`. Because a new slot observation that flips the policy answer satisfies the definitions of both `NEW_EVIDENCE` (*"an admitted issuer supplies a value for a named slot"*) and `NEW_CONSEQUENCE` (*"the same scope gives a different replayed answer"*), the text does not specify whether the novelty function returns a set of labels or a single label under a strict precedence order (e.g., `SCOPE_MISMATCH` $>$ `MIGRATION_REQUIRED` $>$ `NEW_CONSEQUENCE` $>$ `NEW_EVIDENCE` $>$ `NO_NEW_CONSEQUENCE`). Similarly, Section 2.3 (p. 5) classifies *"a budget, a wrapper, a checkpoint"* under `NO_NEW_CONSEQUENCE`, but if re-filing with a larger budget allows a previously `PAUSED` run to finish to `REPLAYED_NORMAL_FORM` (`TRUE` or `FALSE`), it changes the replayed outcome (`NEW_CONSEQUENCE`).
     2. *Equivalence-Preserving Slot Churn (`NEW_EVIDENCE` Without `NEW_CONSEQUENCE`):* If any new or perturbed value supplied by an admitted issuer for a named slot triggers `NEW_EVIDENCE` even when `policy_answer` remains unchanged (`FALSE`), an actor with an admitted issuer can repeatedly submit slightly perturbed observations on the same side of a policy threshold (e.g., measurement jitter or refund amounts of `650.88`, `650.87`, `650.86` against a limit of `500.00`, or updated timestamps) or oscillate between previously rejected values ($A \to B \to A$ if novelty is checked only pairwise) to bypass `NO_NEW_CONSEQUENCE` filtering and force unbounded re-evaluation without representing meaningful progress.
     3. *Unmodeled Dimensions and Schema Blindness Outside Pre-Declared Slots:* Restricting novelty to pre-declared named slots means that epistemic shifts arising from (a) novel defect classes exposed during adversarial review or production failure not anticipated in the recipient's fixed evidence slots (such as the `build.rs` or symlink escape in §4.2, or the TOCTOU snapshot defect in §2.3), (b) changes in the recipient's trust configuration (such as revoking a key for a relied-upon issuer or compiler), or (c) staleness or invalidation of upstream `derived` facts (§2.2) not exposed as top-level named slots fall into `NO_NEW_CONSEQUENCE` (*"an unnamed blob"*) unless a profile migration (`MIGRATION_REQUIRED`) occurs or an explicit escalation path (e.g., routing out-of-schema counterexamples to `questions(scope)`) is provided, and the record indicator that triggers `MIGRATION_REQUIRED` versus `NO_NEW_CONSEQUENCE` is not specified.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Specify the exact input signature (pairwise comparison vs. full scope history) and precedence order of the five novelty outcomes in §2.3 (including clarifying that `NEW_CONSEQUENCE` takes precedence when a budget increase resolves a `PAUSED` state to a normal form), clarify whether `NEW_EVIDENCE` alone (without `NEW_CONSEQUENCE`) can reopen a settled decision, explain how `derived` fact invalidations (§2.2), trust-configuration updates, and schema changes trigger `MIGRATION_REQUIRED`, and note how out-of-schema counterexamples can be routed to `questions(scope)` so critical anomalies are not silently dropped.

### [Evidence Bases and Proof Attestation Architecture] (Pages: 7-8)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Prover, Solver, Environment, and Cross-Crate Escape Vectors in the Creusot Proof Attestation Manifest (§4.2, page 7; Reviewer Question 9):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Section 4.2 states *"Three Rust source files of the verifier are proved with Creusot"* and defines the subject of a signed local Creusot proof attestation checked in CI as `CLAIMED` as a SHA-256 manifest covering "every file of the proof crate, every file its sources name by path, and the Cargo and toolchain configuration of every ancestor directory," cross-checked against `rustc`'s dependency output (`dep-info`) and `git` tracking. While the repairs for `build.rs` and symlinked configuration directories close two file-level escape paths, deductive verification in Creusot relies on several inputs and build contexts outside `rustc`'s file dependency graph:
     - *Prover toolchain and proof-session inputs outside `rustc` `dep-info`:* Verification in Creusot is split between compilation/translation (`creusot-rustc` emitting intermediate Coma/WhyML verification conditions) and obligation discharge (Why3 invoking external SMT solvers such as Z3, cvc5, or Alt-Ergo, or replaying a recorded proof session such as `why3session.xml`). `rustc`'s dependency tracking records only source files read by the Rust compiler frontend; it does not capture the exact `cargo-creusot` or Why3 binary/driver versions, `why3.conf`, external SMT solver binary digests and flags/timeouts, external specifications (`extern_spec!`) loaded outside the `rustc` source graph, or whether the backend solvers actually discharged all generated verification conditions rather than merely completing a compiler pass.
     - *Compile-time environment variables and conditional compilation:* Rust compilation and Creusot contract checking can be altered without modifying any tracked file on disk via environment variables (`RUSTFLAGS`, `CARGO_ENCODED_RUSTFLAGS`, or variables read via `env!`/`option_env!`) and `--cfg` flags passed at invocation time, which are not recorded as file inputs in `rustc`'s `dep-info`.
     - *Cross-crate semantic divergence via `#[path]` mounting:* Compiling the three shipped verifier files through `#[path]` inside a separate proof crate compiles those modules under the *proof crate's* module scope (`crate::...`), Cargo feature set, dependency tree (`Cargo.lock`), and `#[cfg(creusot)]` attributes rather than the production verifier crate's build context. If the shared files contain conditional compilation blocks (e.g., `#[cfg(creusot)]` vs. `#[cfg(not(creusot))]`) or resolve external types/dependencies differently from the shipped crate, the AST proved via `#[path]` can diverge from the compiled production artifact.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify in §4.2 whether the attestation manifest and signing check also bind (a) the exact `cargo-creusot`, Why3, and SMT solver versions/digests and proof session artifacts (`why3session.xml`), (b) active compiler flags (`RUSTFLAGS`, `--cfg` predicates, and feature sets), and (c) the production crate's `Cargo.lock` along with an audit prohibiting diverging `#[cfg(creusot)]` code paths in the three verified files. If external prover binaries and environment flags are not currently pinned in the manifest, explicitly note them as residual trust assumptions of a `CLAIMED` attestation to make its exact guarantee transparent. Additionally, clarify which of the four bases in §4.1 the Creusot proof attestation exemplifies (`actor attestation` vs. `runner attestation`) and how the CI status label `CLAIMED` maps onto the basis vocabulary of §4.1 and §4.3.

2. **Operational Asymmetry, Policy-Downgrade Incentives, Oracle Input Laundering, and Unbridged Replay Verification in WRT-021 (§4.1 and §4.3, pages 7–8; Reviewer Question 7):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Section 4.1 establishes four evidence bases (`actor attestation`, `runner attestation`, `recipient re-execution`, and `deterministic replay`) under the rule "never upgrade one into another," and Section 4.3 explains that because the WRT-021 profile currently validates only `actor attestation` of resolvable evidence, any transition candidate claiming a stronger basis is reported as `UNVERIFIED_BASIS` and closes nothing. Directly related to Reviewer Question 7 (page 12, regarding where the weakest link lies when the cheapest basis is used most), this mechanism introduces four structural pressures:
     - *Incentive toward policy and claim downgrading under authoring friction:* Because writing machine-readable WPL policies and replay validators is high-friction (as shown by 23 of 24 repository pairs being `OPAQUE` in §2.1) and a transition that truthfully claims `runner attestation` (backed by an actual probe or CI run) or `REPLAYED` is rejected as `UNVERIFIED_BASIS` and leaves its obligation `OPEN`—whereas a transition claiming the weaker `actor attestation` over the exact same underlying artifact is validated and admitted whenever the duty's policy permits actor attestations—producers and policy authors face strong convenience pressure to downgrade duty admission policies to accept `actor attestation` and wrap runner logs or replay receipts inside `actor attestation` claims so that workflows are not blocked. Once stronger evidence is routinely labeled as `actor attestation` to pass validation, the four-tier separation collapses in stored records.
     - *Oracle / input boundary laundering (`evidence -> facts`, §2.2 & §4.1):* Even when a policy computation is verified via the strongest basis (`deterministic replay`), its leaf inputs (`observed` facts) almost always enter the store via the weakest basis (`actor attestation`). If a profile or UI reports a transition's overall basis as `REPLAYED` without surfacing that the inputs fed into that reduction were self-attested by the same actor, the separation collapses in practice.
     - *Collapse of runner attestation into actor attestation under single-operator keys:* As noted in Section 8 ("One operator, many keys"), if the runner signing key and the agent signing key reside in the same operator environment without hardware or third-party isolation, a `runner attestation` reduces security-wise to an `actor attestation`, and an actor can also sign an `actor attestation` whose prose payload claims *"I ran deterministic replay X and it passed."*
     - *Unexplained disconnection from existing deterministic replay verifiers:* Because `warrant` (`ski@v1`, §2.1 and §2.3) and `sigma-resume` (§3) already implement deterministic replay verifiers in the same repository ecosystem, it is unclear from §4.3 why WRT-021 treats `REPLAYED` (`deterministic replay`) as completely unvalidated (`UNVERIFIED_BASIS`) rather than invoking the existing replay verifier when a transition cites a replayable computation record.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** First, explain in §4.3 why WRT-021 does not yet delegate `REPLAYED` claims to the existing `ski@v1` / `sigma-resume` replay verifiers (e.g., whether this is an unimplemented integration hook or a record-format gap), and specify what concrete verification criteria and key-isolation requirements separate a verifiable `runner attestation` from an `actor attestation` over a structured run log signed by a runner-scoped key. Second, to prevent producers from mislabeling stronger evidence as `actor attestation` while validators are incomplete, clarify whether an assessment can record both a target `claimed_basis` (e.g., `runner_attestation` or `REPLAYED`) and a verified floor `assessed_basis` (`actor_attestation`) when a valid actor signature is present—keeping duties that strictly require the stronger basis `OPEN` (`UNVERIFIED_BASIS`), while not penalizing honest basis claims on duties whose policies admit actor attestation. Third, consider tracking the effective evidence basis of any assessment or closure as a tuple `(computation_basis, min(input_fact_bases))` so that self-attested leaf inputs are never masked by a `REPLAYED` computation label.

3. **Partial Ordering vs. Linear Hierarchy and View-Local Semantics Across the Four Evidence Bases (§4.1 and §4.3, pages 7–8):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Section 4.1 frames the defect as "a check that reports a weaker kind of evidence under the name of a stronger one," and Section 4.3 refers to "Candidates claiming any stronger basis [than actor attestation]." This phrasing implies a single linear hierarchy across the four bases in which any basis can be asserted by a producer in a transition candidate. However, two structural distinctions are left implicit:
     - *Incomparability between pure replay and empirical execution:* `deterministic replay` (reducing a pure, portable combinator term to normal form) and `runner attestation` / `recipient re-execution` (which can execute impure, host-observing commands such as the service-recovery probe in §4.3) apply to different computation classes and form a partial order rather than a total linear order. `deterministic replay` is stronger than `actor attestation` for pure policy evaluation, but it cannot observe live external state where a `runner attestation` or `recipient re-execution` is required.
     - *Producer-claimed vs. evaluator-performed bases across multi-agent views:* In Table 4.1, `recipient re-execution` ("the recipient ran it again, under its own policy") is listed alongside `actor attestation`, `runner attestation`, and `deterministic replay`. However, a transition record is authored by a producer submitting a closure candidate prior to the recipient's evaluation, and state in this architecture is computed per recipient under a named view (§2.3, §5.4). When a reviewer re-executes a check and records that support in the store (as in the first specimen in §5.5, which notes that *"the support is recorded but never raises a basis"*), while the reviewer experiences `recipient re-execution` locally, to any third-party reader evaluating the store under its own view, that stored record is structurally an `actor attestation` (or unverified runner claim) by the reviewer, not a `recipient re-execution` by the new reader. Grouping `recipient re-execution` in the same flat candidate-claim ladder leaves unclear whether a producer transition can ever legitimately claim `recipient re-execution` or whether it is strictly a view-local basis established during the assessing recipient's own evaluation.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify in §4.1 (and cross-reference in §5.5) that the four bases form a partial order (distinguishing pure term reduction from empirical host observation) rather than a single linear hierarchy, and explicitly distinguish which bases can be claimed in a stored producer candidate versus which bases (`recipient re-execution`, and local verification of `deterministic replay`) are established view-locally by the assessing recipient—explaining that a recorded citation of an acceptor's re-execution never raises the store-wide assessed basis because it constitutes only an actor attestation to third-party readers.

### [Open Loops Profile (WRT-021 Draft) and Dogfood Specimens] (Pages: 8-10)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Potential Concern: Vulnerability of Policy-Mediated Duty Instantiation to Indirect Obligation Proliferation (§5.1 & §5.4, Pages 8–9; Reviewer Question 1)**
   - **Observation:** Section 5.1 separates `Claim`, `Question`, and `Duty`, stating: *"A question becomes a duty only through an act by someone with authority over the holder: the holder itself, or a policy the holder is under. Without this rule, every conversation installs unbounded work on everyone in it."* Section 5.4 further introduces `questions(scope)` as *"a shared, read-only view of unowned questions, with their provenance."* (This directly connects to Reviewer Question 1 in §9.)
   - **Analysis:** While separating `Claim`, `Question`, and `Duty` stops unauthenticated external prose from directly injecting duties into an actor's frontier, it is not sufficient on its own when the holder operates under a standing reactive policy or `purpose` (*"a generator of other loops that is never itself discharged"*). In automated multi-agent engineering workflows (such as the repository governance practices described in §6.2), actors commonly operate under standing reactive policies—for example, a standing rule that any blocking review finding, `AMEND` request, regression counterexample, or failed check in a monitored scope automatically generates a triage or remediation duty for the scope's designated maintainer. If an unprivileged, noisy, or generative actor can cheaply emit transitions that open `Question` records or PRs matching the trigger predicate of such a standing policy, the holder's own policy supplies the persistence authority to convert that unbounded stream of epistemic demands into binding `Duty` records on the holder's `frontier(actor)` without an explicit per-duty acceptance act. Moreover, even when items remain in `questions(scope)` or as pending delegation requests, if release governance (§6.2) requires a maintainer or triage loop to scan and triage the unowned question queue before release, the triage obligation itself grows unboundedly.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify in §5.1 how policy-triggered duty creation is bounded in WRT-021. Specifically, consider specifying whether auto-assigning policies require:
     1. Restricting trigger eligibility to questions/events signed by a closed set of admitted issuers in the recipient's trust snapshot (and similarly clarifying whether `questions(scope)` in §5.4 is filtered by the view's trust snapshot);
     2. Enforcing concurrency, rate, quota, or budget caps on active policy-generated duties per holder/scope (queuing excess demands); or
     3. Instantiating externally triggered demands into a bounded intake/triage transition (or bundling multiple findings from a single review round into one composite intake duty) that requires an explicit holder admission checkpoint before expanding the active obligation frontier.

2. **Potential Concern: Expressiveness Gaps in the Duty Taxonomy Regarding Institutional Power and Prohibitions (§5.1, §5.3 & §5.4, Pages 8–9; Reviewer Question 2)**
   - **Observation:** Section 5.1 restricts the taxonomy of loops to four duty kinds (`goal`, `commitment`, `obligation`, and `purpose`, with only `obligation` implemented) and asks in Reviewer Question 2 (§9) whether deontic concepts such as permission, prohibition, or power are missing for a record layer. Meanwhile, §5.1, §5.3, and §6.2 rely extensively on rules governing *who can alter normative state*: who has *"authority over the holder"* (§5.1), whose acceptance is required under a duty's admission policy (§5.3), who may initiate a transfer (*"Only the holder transfers"*), when creditor consent is required (*"a substitution also needs the creditor"*), and that a closure or key *"cannot enlarge the delegation"* (§6.2).
   - **Analysis:** While excluding mentalistic states (`desire`, `value`, `meaning`) is well-justified for a cryptographic record layer and point-in-time `permission` is already handled at the request-decision/assessment layer (`consumer_admits_action` in §2.3 or `released` exemptions) without needing to be a loop kind, two normative primitives are already implicitly required and operating in Section 5's mechanics without explicit representation in the record schema:
     1. *Institutional Power / Competence (Hohfeldian Power):* In normative multi-agent systems, the ability of an actor's signed record to instantiate, release, transfer, or admit the discharge of another actor's duty is an exercise of *institutional power* (or *competence*), distinct from a duty. Without a first-class record representation of power/authority (or an explicit schema in the recipient's trust configuration defining who is empowered to create, delegate, or release a duty in a given scope), an evaluator cannot mechanically check from the record layer whether a duty-creation or release transition is authorized, or who holds the competence to forcefully revoke/reassign a stalled duty when a holder key is lost or an agent defaults into a `LAPSED` state (where the strict rule *"Only the holder transfers"* would otherwise block creditor-side recovery).
     2. *Prohibitions vs. Positive Maintenance Invariants:* Section 5.1 defines a duty as a loop *"to answer a question, reach a state or keep a condition,"* and §5.4/§5.5 (Specimen 2) model keeping a condition (*"service functional"*) via periodic fresh observations (`observed` disposition) that decay to `UNOBSERVED` or `LAPSED` when stale. However, "keeping a condition" conflates *positive liveness invariants* (monitored via freshness probes) with event-driven *negative prohibitions / safety constraints* (e.g., *"never modify files outside scope $X$"* or *"never merge to `main` without a regression record"*). A prohibition does not decay to `UNOBSERVED` simply because a periodic timer elapsed; rather, it holds continuously across revisions until a single contrary-to-duty counterexample record is observed in the store, which immediately transitions the constraint to a violated/contradicted state.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly formalize *power/authority bindings* in the WRT-021 duty schema—pinning the cryptographic identities or threshold policies holding assigner (persistence), creditor (release/substitution, including recovery/reassignment competence upon `LAPSED`), and adjudicator/acceptor authority—and distinguish between *observed state invariants* (which require fresh probes and decay to `UNOBSERVED`) and *transition prohibitions/constraints* (which transition to a `violated` or `contradicted` state distinct from `LAPSED` upon a counterexample transition).

3. **Potential Concern: Static vs. Dynamic Named-Child Closure Conditions, Upward Contradiction Propagation, and Downward `ORPHANED` Semantics (§5.3, §5.4 & §5.5, Pages 9–10; Reviewer Question 3)**
   - **Observation:** Section 5.3 establishes the locality rule for typed closure: *"Closing a child never closes the parent it serves, even when it is the only child. A parent may name children in its own closure condition. That condition is evaluated on the parent's own candidate and is never inferred from structure."* Section 5.4 adds that *"An unknown reference is MISSING, never open, satisfied or orphaned. A root duty is never vacuously ORPHANED,"* and §5.5 notes that *"A parent's condition over its children was implicit. This was fixed with named children."* (This engages Reviewer Question 3 in §9.)
   - **Analysis:** Prohibiting implicit upward discharge propagation is sound for upward sufficiency (preventing premature parent closure when task decomposition is not exhaustive, as shown in §5.5 Specimen 1). However, three operational mechanics of parent-child loops merit explicit handling for practice and independent reimplementation:
     1. *Dynamic fan-out vs. content addressing and derived readiness:* Because Warrant records are immutable and content-addressed by their canonical SHA-256 bytes (§1.1), a parent duty (such as a review round in Specimen 1 or an incident recovery in Specimen 2) is typically created *before* the transitions that discover and spawn specific blocking findings (child duties). At the time the parent duty is hashed and signed, the SHA-256 hashes of not-yet-created child duties do not exist, making static naming at parent creation time brittle. Furthermore, requiring a separate transaction after the last child closes adds latency if the parent holder is offline. It is unclear whether binding dynamically discovered children requires issuing a transition that amends/supersedes the parent's closure condition on every task breakdown, whether children are enumerated via symbolic slot identifiers or scoped predicates (and how such predicates avoid premature satisfaction under `UNATTESTED` view completeness when a child creation record is withheld), or whether a single `transition` record (§5.2) can include closure mappings for both the last child and the parent simultaneously (or report a derived `READY_TO_CLOSE` status when all named children are discharged).
     2. *Upward contradiction / invalidation propagation in `reliance(duty)`:* While *discharge* must not propagate upward implicitly, **invalidation, reopening, or staleness** *must* propagate upward: if a parent closure candidate $D_{\text{parent}}$ was admitted because its named children $C_1, C_2$ were discharged at revision $R_1$, and $C_1$'s closure is later reopened, contested, or found to be `CONTRADICTED` at revision $R_2$, it is unclear whether `reliance(parent_duty)` automatically degrades when a named child loses reliability, or whether strict non-propagation leaves the parent `RELIABLE`.
     3. *Downward cancellation / release and the `ORPHANED` state:* While upward propagation is forbidden and §5.4 states that a root duty is never vacuously `ORPHANED`, strict non-propagation when a parent duty is `released` or `declared impossible` leaves all of its spawned child duties open in their holders' frontiers (`frontier(actor)`), creating orphaned work. The text does not define the exact state-transition rule that causes a non-root child duty to become `ORPHANED` (e.g., whether `ORPHANED` occurs when a parent duty record is `MISSING` from the store snapshot, or whether downward release propagation / a `SUPERSEDED_BY_PARENT` or `ORPHANED` flag applies when a parent duty is `released` or `declared impossible`).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Specify in §5.3–§5.4: (a) how a parent duty's closure condition binds to child duties spawned dynamically after the parent's creation (and whether a single transition can close both the final child and the parent, or surface a `READY_TO_CLOSE` indicator); (b) that `reliance(parent_duty)` transitively re-verifies the current uncontested reliability of all named children so upward invalidation propagates; and (c) the exact condition that transitions a non-root duty to `ORPHANED` (including downward cancellation when a parent is `released` or `declared impossible`).

4. **Potential Concern: Underspecified Liability Distinction Between `delegated` and `substituted` Dispositions and Pending Handshakes (§5.3 & §5.4, Page 9)**
   - **Observation:** Section 5.3 lists `delegated` and `substituted` alongside `discharged`, `released`, `declared impossible`, and `observed` as the six closure dispositions, and states: *"Only the holder transfers. The incoming actor must accept that exact transfer, and a substitution also needs the creditor. A delegation is a request until the delegate accepts."* Section 5.4 then defines `frontier(actor)` as *"the duties it holds, is delegated, is owed, may release or must accept that are open or flagged."*
   - **Analysis:** Grouping `delegated` and `substituted` as closure dispositions without a formal state-transition specification leaves two accountability ambiguities:
     1. *Debtor liability retention under delegation vs. substitution:* In multi-agent commitment ontologies (such as Singh 1999, cited in §7), *substitution* (novation) extinguishes the original holder's obligation and replaces the debtor with the incoming actor—which is why creditor consent is mandatory. By contrast, *delegation* does not require creditor consent because it subcontracts execution to a delegate while the original holder retains fallback accountability to the creditor if the delegate defaults or lapses. Because §5.3 treats both `delegated` and `substituted` as closure dispositions on a duty, it is unclear whether an admitted `delegated` disposition removes the duty from the original holder's active obligation set in `frontier(actor)`. If `delegated` closes the original holder's duty on par with `substituted`, a debtor could unilaterally shed accountability to a consenting, untrusted delegate without the creditor's agreement, bypassing the creditor-consent requirement.
     2. *Pending, rejected, or defaulted handoffs:* While a transfer or delegation request awaits acceptance (`must accept` on the incoming actor's frontier), or if the incoming actor/creditor rejects the request, or if an accepted delegate subsequently defaults (`LAPSED` or `declared impossible`), the text does not specify how the request expires/cancels or how the duty's state reverts on the original holder's frontier.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly distinguish the post-admission state of the original duty under `delegated` versus `substituted` in `frontier(actor)` and `reliance(duty)`—confirming that the original holder remains residually accountable to the creditor under a `delegated` disposition until the underlying obligation is uncontestedly `discharged` or `released`—and define how pending, rejected, or defaulted delegations are resolved.

5. **Potential Concern: View Divergence in Multi-Party Handshakes, Contestation Standing, and Revision Pinning in `reliance(duty)` (§5.4 & §5.5, Pages 9–10; Reviewer Question 4)**
   - **Observation:** Section 5.4 defines every assessment relative to a recipient's local `view` (one snapshot of the records and blobs, the recipient's trust snapshot, an `as_of` time, the evaluator's digest, and `UNATTESTED` completeness) and states that `reliance(duty)` *"says RELIABLE only for an uncontested discharge established on a revision the recipient treats as current."* Section 5.5 (Specimen 1) notes that *"The closure is UNASSESSED for the final revision until revalidated"* and *"Applicability followed the revision where a defect was found, not the one where its fix was established. This was fixed."* (This engages Reviewer Question 4 in §9.)
   - **Analysis:** Computing duty state per recipient under a named local `view` is a sound foundation for asynchronous, offline, or mutually distrusting agents when a single consumer checks whether it may rely on a closure. However, three operational aspects of this model warrant tighter specification for multi-agent coordination:
     1. *Split-brain frontiers during bilateral transfers and multi-party settlement gates:* Operations such as bilateral transfer, delegation, substitution (§5.3), and multi-party release governance (§6.2) require two or three parties to coordinate. Because view completeness is `UNATTESTED` (§8), if Holder $A$ and Incoming Actor $B$ (or Creditor $C$) evaluate `frontier(actor)` over slightly different local views ($V_A \neq V_B$ due to store sync lag, withheld records, differing trust snapshots, or `as_of` cutoffs), $A$ may compute that a delegation acceptance or discharge was admitted and stop working, while $B$ or $C$ sees a subsequent reopening or misses the acceptance, leaving the obligation silently unworked. Coordination does not require a single mutable global state, but irreversible joint transitions do require view convergence.
     2. *Definition and standing of "uncontested discharge":* Sections 5.2–5.4 do not specify what record type or transition field constitutes a valid "contest" or "reopening" of a discharge, nor who holds the authority/standing to contest a discharge so that `reliance(duty)` ceases to be `RELIABLE`. Without a standing and evidentiary rule, any unprivileged actor could cause a denial of settlement by filing a spurious contesting record against a closed duty.
     3. *Target revision parameterization and orthogonal commits:* While `reliance(duty)` depends on *"a revision the recipient treats as current,"* the formal `view` definition in §5.4 does not include the target code/artifact revision hash (or scope-to-revision map). Furthermore, if revision currency requires strict equality with the repository's global commit hash rather than a scoped tree/manifest hash, any subsequent commit in an unrelated directory or scope would immediately downgrade all prior settled discharges to `UNASSESSED`.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** (a) Define the exact record mechanism and actor standing required to contest or reopen a discharged duty; (b) explicitly include the target revision (or scope-to-revision mapping) in the formal `view` parameters in §5.4 and explain how revision matching handles orthogonal commits; and (c) for bilateral transfers and multi-party settlement gates, introduce mutually co-signed view-agreement checkpoints (where interacting actors pin and co-sign the view tuple `(store_snapshot_hash, trust_snapshot_hash, as_of, evaluator_digest)` and resulting `frontier` digest) so counterparties can deterministically evaluate `frontier(counterparty, pinned_view)` and detect view divergence before executing an irreversible joint transition.

### [Multi-Agent Settlement Vision, Related Work, Threat Model, and Reviewer Questions] (Pages: 10-14)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Vulnerability of Result Capsules and False-Compression Guards to Multi-Identity Adversaries, Transitive Assumption Laundering, and Trigger Ambiguity (Section 6.1, p. 10; Section 9, Question 8, p. 12)**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section 6.1, the proposed `result capsule` representation introduces two rules to guard against false compression: (1) *"Repetition earns no validation credit. A capsule re-asserted ten times is one capsule,"* and (2) *"Shared assumptions stay visible. A lemma many results reuse carries its open assumptions into every consumer's frontier."* As anticipated in Section 9 (Question 8), there are four structural gaps in how these rules behave against an adversary—or a convenience-driven operator—controlling multiple identities or chaining capsules:
     - *Structural Wrapping vs. Content-Addressed Deduplication (Rule 1):* If a capsule's identity is the SHA-256 hash of its canonical bytes (following the `warrant` record format), deduplicating byte-identical records does not prevent an adversary with $k$ keys from generating $k$ distinct capsule records for the same claim by varying the signer key, `as_of` timestamp, `view` snapshot, or wrapper phrasing. Unless "one capsule" is equivalence-classed by a canonical tuple—such as `(scope, claim_hash, assessed_evidence_basis, root_grounds_set)`—and bounded by the *admitted issuer set* in the recipient's trust configuration rather than raw capsule or key count, multi-identity repetition will bypass Rule 1.
     - *Transitive Assumption Laundering vs. On-Demand Fetching (Rule 2):* Section 6.1 states that an agent *"continues from the capsules and the residual frontier, and fetches the evidence on demand."* If an adversary chains capsules ($C_1 \to C_2 \to \dots \to C_n$) across distinct identities where $C_{i+1}$ cites $C_i$ in `its grounds, as addresses` while omitting $C_i$'s open assumptions from its own top-level `the assumptions that remain` field, lazy on-demand fetching will hide those upstream assumptions from a consumer of $C_n$. Conversely, if the verifier must recursively traverse the entire ground DAG to verify that no open assumption or `losses` entry was dropped, the capsule loses its constant-size lookup advantage unless capsule admission requires a machine-checkable transitive union (or Merkle summary) of unresolved upstream assumptions.
     - *Frontier Pollution / Denial-of-Service (Rule 2):* Because Rule 2 dictates that a reused lemma carries all of its open assumptions into every consumer's frontier, a negligent or adversarial agent can attach numerous trivial or unresolvable open assumptions to a widely reused foundational lemma, inflating `frontier(actor)` across all downstream consumers and defeating the anti-entropy objective. Furthermore, propagating explicit assumptions does not surface *implicit* shared dependencies (such as relying on the same unverified runner binary or single-operator environment noted in Section 8).
     - *Mechanical Evaluation of Invalidation Triggers:* The seventh field of a result capsule (`the changes that should trigger re-examination`) is currently unspecified. If triggers are recorded as prose descriptions rather than content-addressed slots, revision bindings, or WPL predicates over named evidence slots (§2.3), an agent cannot mechanically determine whether a capsule remains `RELIABLE` or has become `STALE` under a new `view` without re-reading the history.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Specify in Section 6.1 that (a) validation credit is ordered strictly by the *assessed evidence basis* (§4.1) and the recipient profile's *admitted trust roles* rather than the count of distinct signing keys or capsule hashes; (b) a capsule's open-assumption set must be verified at admission time as a superset of its grounds' unresolved assumptions, paired with explicit consumer consent/scoping upon import (analogous to duty transfer in §5.3) to prevent frontier flooding; and (c) re-examination triggers must reference a closed, machine-checkable vocabulary (pinned file/policy hashes, named evidence slots from §2.3, or specific duty IDs whose disposition must remain `discharged`).

2. **Characterization of Prior Art on Decentralized Commitment Views and Ongoing Obligations in Related Work (Section 7, p. 11; Section 9, Question 4, p. 12)**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section 7, the comparison with multi-agent commitment ontologies and policy engines has two potential gaps in how prior literature is characterized:
     - *Local-View Commitment Computation:* Under *Commitments in multi-agent systems*, the paper cites only Singh (1999) and defends as one of three core differentiators that *"the state is computed per recipient, under a named view, rather than held as a shared truth."* While accurate relative to Singh (1999), subsequent work in the multi-agent commitment literature by Singh and Chopra—specifically Information Protocols (BSPL; Singh, 2011), *Cupid* (Chopra and Singh, 2015), and *Custard* (Chopra and Singh, 2016)—explicitly moved multi-agent commitments away from shared global state to decentralized computation over each agent's local view of immutable event logs. Citing this later line of work would make Section 7 more accurate while sharpening the paper's actual differentiating contribution, which is strictly **evidential** (cryptographic content-addressing, explicit separation of the four evidence bases, and typed non-propagating closure).
     - *Ongoing Obligations and Stateful Usage Control:* Under *Provenance and policy engines*, the paper contrasts its approach only with stateless point-in-time request engines (OPA and Cedar). In access and usage control literature, stateful obligation models such as $\text{UCON}_{\text{ABC}}$ (Park and Sandhu, 2004) and the OASIS XACML v3.0 standard natively model ongoing obligations, pre-/post-conditions, and four-valued decision results (`Permit`, `Deny`, `Indeterminate`, `NotApplicable`) paired with mandatory `Obligations`. Contrasting WRT-021 and the typed assessment profile (§2.3) against $\text{UCON}_{\text{ABC}}$ and XACML would clarify how binding policies to portable combinator reductions and verifying discharge evidence per recipient view advances beyond prior stateful obligation architectures.

3. **Converse Goodhart Risks in Proposed Observability Quantities (Section 6.3, p. 11)**
   - **Potential Concern:** Section 6.3 rightly rejects optimizing a global ratio of closed-to-open loops, citing Strathern (1997). However, several of the five proposed alternative quantities (*"reopenings and their reasons"*, *"capsule reuse"*, *"the repeated-review cost"*, *"newly exposed assumptions"*, and *"concrete duties settled"*) remain vulnerable to converse Goodhart effects if treated as optimization targets by autonomous agents:
     - Rewarding *"newly exposed assumptions"* can incentivize agents to fragment a single missing premise into numerous synthetic questions or assumptions.
     - Rewarding *"capsule reuse"* can incentivize premature settlement of trivial lemmas just to cite them downstream.
     - Penalizing *"reopenings"* or *"repeated-review cost"* can discourage agents from filing legitimate reopenings when new counterexamples emerge, pushing disagreements back into unrecorded chat channels.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify in Section 6.3 that these five quantities are diagnostic telemetry rather than optimization targets, or pair them as tensioned metrics (for example, weighting *"newly exposed assumptions"* by whether they subsequently drive a `NEW_CONSEQUENCE` or `NEW_EVIDENCE` transition under Section 2.3).
