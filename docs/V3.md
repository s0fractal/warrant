# Warrant 3 — engineering specification (draft)

**Status:** draft, not adopted. Warrant 2 (`SPEC.md`, `warrant/`, `.warrant/store/`) stays the
current format until a switch is decided; this document and `warrant3/` sit beside it. Key words
MUST / MUST NOT / MAY per RFC 2119.

Warrant 3 is one small core over one record envelope. The domain rules that Warrant 2's
repository tools re-implement three times (claims, roadmap, reviews: lineage, forks, acceptor
terms, acceptance indexes) become one lineage rule in the core and data in the store: schema
records. What a receiver relies on is still its own verdict, under its own trust and profile.

## 1. Encoding

As Warrant 2 §2: JSON values without floats, integers within ±(2^53−1), no lone surrogates;
canonical bytes are RFC 8785 (JCS); hashes are lowercase hex SHA-256; time is integer Unix seconds.
The implementation reuses Warrant 2's `jcs.py` and `keys.py` unchanged.

## 2. The record

```json
{"warrant": "3", "kind": K, "actor": A, "at": T, "prev": ID|null, "schema": NAME|null,
 "lineage": ID|null, "refs": [HEX64…], "guard": GUARD|null, "body": {…}}
```

All ten members are required; no other member is allowed. The record's **id** is
`sha256(canon(record))`. A record is stored as the canonical line
`canon({"record": R, "sig": S})`, where `S` is `{"key": <key id>, "ed25519": <hex128>}` over the
bytes `"warrant-sig-v3:" || id_raw`, or `null` for a migrated record (§9). The v3 domain prefix
differs from v2's `warrant-sig-v1:`: no v2 signature verifies as a v3 one.

- `actor`: an actor id as in Warrant 2 §3 (NFC, ≤ 200 code points, no control character, no
  surrounding white space).
- `prev`: the id of this actor's previous signed record, or null for its first (§7).
- `refs`: a sorted list of distinct hex64: the records and blobs this record depends on. A body
  field of a reference type (§4) MUST also appear in `refs`. The graph's edges are read from
  `refs`, `lineage`, `guard.requires` and `prev`, never from free text.
- `body`: an object; its canonical bytes are at most **16 384** bytes. Larger content is a
  blob (§8) named in `refs`.
- `guard`: §6.

**Kinds** (four):

| kind | says | `schema` | `lineage` | `body.target` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `schema` | the rules of one record schema (§4) | null | an earlier version of the same schema | — |
| `entity` | one version of a thing: a claim, a goal, an obligation, a pull request | required | the version it replaces, or null | — |
| `attest` | an actor's statement about one record: acceptance, a replay result, an observation | optional | null | the record attested |
| `challenge` | a counterexample or objection against one record | optional | null | the record challenged |

An `attest` or `challenge` MUST name its target in `body.target` (also in `refs`; `refs` is sorted,
so no position in it can name the target) and have a null `lineage` and `guard`.

## 3. Lineage

A record whose `lineage` is L **succeeds** L. L MUST be in the view, of the same kind and the same
schema; otherwise the record is **excluded** from every lineage (`LINEAGE_BROKEN`). The records
reachable through `lineage` from a record with `lineage: null` (its **root**) form the root's
lineage; the lineage is named by its root's id. Because an id covers `lineage`, a version is
always newer than what it names: lineages are acyclic by construction, and no clock orders them.

- **Who may succeed.** A successor's actor MUST be the root's actor, or hold a role the schema
  lists in `successors` under the receiver's profile (§10). Otherwise it is excluded
  (`LINEAGE_NOT_AUTHOR`). This one rule replaces `LINEAGE_NOT_AUTHOR` (claims),
  `REVIEW_HEAD_FILER` (reviews) and the author check of roadmap revisions.
- **FORK** is a core verdict. Two admitted records succeeding the same record fork the lineage:
  the lineage's verdict is `FORK`, every branch is listed, and no record is excluded for it (as
  Warrant 2 §4 keeps both branches of a chain fork: exclusion would let a forker retract its own
  earlier records). A forked lineage has no leaf and no state. This replaces `FORK`,
  `GOAL_FORK`, `REVIEW_HEAD_FORK` and `FINDING_FORK`.
- **Leaf**: the one version with no admitted successor. **Effective version**: the newest version
  on the path from the root to the leaf whose guard is met or absent (§6). The lineage's
  **state** is the effective version's `body.state` (null when none is effective). The leaf
  being pending while an earlier version is effective is how a proposal waits: a closure that
  was admitted stays history (Warrant 2 §4.3), and a reformulation that is not yet accepted
  changes nothing.
- A successor dated before what it succeeds is listed (`LINEAGE_BACKDATED`) and still counts:
  the id, not `at`, witnesses the order.

## 4. Schemas are records

A `schema` record's body:

```json
{"name": "claim@1",
 "fields": {"state": "str", "text": "str", "witnesses": "strs", "reason": "str?", "answers": "ref"},
 "states": {"unbacked": null, "backed": GUARD},
 "closed": ["backed"],
 "successors": []}
```

- `fields`: the closed set of body members and their types: `str`, `int`, `bool`, `strs` (list
  of strings), `ref` (hex64), `refs` (list of hex64), `obj` (any JSON value); a trailing `?` makes
  the member optional and nullable. Members of type `ref`/`refs` MUST appear in the record's `refs`.
- `states` (entities only): the allowed values of `body.state`, each with the **minimal guard**
  a version entering that state MUST carry (null: none).
- `closed`: the states that close the lineage for `guard.requires` (§6).
- `successors`: roles that may succeed a version besides the root's actor.

A body that does not fit its schema makes the record `INVALID` (listed, counted nowhere). A
schema record counts only when its actor holds the role `schema/author` in the receiver's
profile; the effective version of a schema lineage is used. Two counted schema lineages naming
the same `name` are `SCHEMA_CONFLICT`, and every record of that name is invalid until one is
retired. Schemas are resolved over the whole view, not by position. The name `migration@1` is
reserved by the core (§9).

`warrant3 bootstrap` files the schemas `claim@1`, `goal@1`, `obligation@1`, `pr-head@1` and
`finding@1` (`warrant3/bootstrap.py`). A new domain is a new schema record; it needs no code.

## 5. Attest and challenge

An `attest` by actor A **on** record X (its `body.target`) states A's position on exactly X's bytes:
because X's id covers its evidence, accepting a version accepts exactly its evidence set
(Warrant 2 §3.3's binding, by construction). An acceptance's body is `{"verdict": "accept"}`; a
replay result's is `{"verdict": "reproduces" | "does_not_reproduce" | "no_result" |
"does_not_apply", "on": <version id>}`. An attest names the record it attests, so it is always
newer than it; an attest by X's own actor never counts toward X's guard.

A `challenge` against X **counts** when its actor is X's actor, holds a role in X's guard, or
holds the profile role `challenger`; or when it is **reproduced**: an actor holding the role
`replayer` attests `reproduces` on the challenge with `on` equal to X. Whoever filed a
reproduced challenge does not matter (Warrant 2 §4.4's evidence-weighted challenge, without the
observe condition). A counted challenge **stands** until a version after X in its lineage is
effective (an X in no lineage: always); a successor answers it once it is accepted, as Warrant 2's
revalidation does. Challenges never rewrite a guard verdict: they lower
reliance (§10), as in Warrant 2 §4.3.

## 6. Guard

```json
{"quorum": N, "roles": [ROLE…], "match": {FIELD: VALUE…}, "requires": [LINEAGE…]}
```

All four members are required. A version's guard is **met** iff

1. at least `quorum` distinct actors, none of them the version's actor, each holding a role in
   `roles` under the receiver's profile, filed an authentic `attest` on the version whose body
   has every `match` member with an equal value; and
2. if the version's state is in its schema's `closed`, every lineage in the accumulated
   `requires` (this version's and every earlier version's in its lineage: requires only grow)
   has an effective state in its own schema's `closed`.

Otherwise it is `PENDING`, with what it waits for. A version without a guard is effective as
filed. A version whose guard is weaker than its schema's minimal guard for its state
(smaller quorum, a role outside the schema's, a `match` member missing or different, a missing
`requires`) is `INVALID`.

One guard replaces `attest`, `observe`, `compute` and the claims tool's `by_replay`. Who
attests decides what the attestation is worth: a person with a role, or a **checker** — an actor
named `checker sha256:<D>` where D is the digest of everything that decides its output. D MUST
cover the checker's whole import closure (every module it loads from its repository, refused
when one lies outside the hashed tree), not one file: a partial identity is the minds
`H_LENS_KERNEL` lesson (ASSURANCE.md: the host decided bytes the kernel hash did not name), and
Warrant 2's G4 replay checker names only `replay.py`'s bytes while importing the claims library
(mitigated there by re-executing the reported commit). "Replayed" stays a verifiable property:
anyone may re-run the code D names and compare.

## 7. Actor chains

Each actor's signed records form its chain through `prev` (Warrant 2 F43). A record is chained
iff its `prev` is null and it is that actor's only genesis, or `prev` names a signed record of the
same actor in the view not dated after it. Two records naming the same `prev`, or two genesis
records of one actor, are a chain fork: listed, nothing excluded. A writer takes `prev` from its
head (the chained record no other names) under an exclusive lock on `records.jsonl`, and refuses
to date a record before it; `--expect-head` makes the append a compare-and-swap. Migrated
records (`sig: null`) are in no chain. No rule of §§3–6 orders records by `at`; chains are
evidence of each writer's own order.

## 8. Store

`.warrant/records.jsonl`: one canonical line per record, `\n`-terminated. `.warrant/blobs/<sha256>`:
blobs, each named by the hash of its bytes; only content that does not fit a body (> 16 384 bytes)
or is not JSON. `.gitattributes` sets `.warrant/records.jsonl merge=union`.

- **Canonical order:** distinct lines sorted by record id. `warrant3 fmt` rewrites the file in
  it; `warrant3 assess` reports `canonical: false` for any other order and CI refuses that.
- **Union merge:** two branches that append records merge without conflict. The reader accepts
  any order and identical duplicate lines (counted once). Two different lines with the same id
  differ only in `sig` (the id covers the rest): the record is authentic if either line is, and
  the pair is listed (`DUPLICATE_ID`). A line that is not canonical, whose id does not match, or
  that exceeds 65 536 bytes is rejected and listed; it never aborts the read.
- **Snapshot digest:** SHA-256 over the sorted distinct lines and the sorted blob names with
  their digests. Moving the bytes elsewhere does not change it.
- **Limits:** records, bytes, refs per record and lineage depth are bounded (`warrant3/store.py`
  `LIMITS`); a store beyond a limit is refused before it is assessed. The core is a pure function
  over bounded input; an agent that needs a wall-clock bound still runs it in a subprocess, as
  Warrant 2's `warrant_agent` views do.

## 9. Migration from Warrant 2, and authenticity

`tools/migrate_v2_to_v3.py` converts a v2 store deterministically: the same v2 store gives the
same v3 record ids, byte for byte, whoever runs it.

- A v2 signature covers a v2 id; it cannot carry over to a v3 envelope, and the migrator MUST
  NOT sign as the v2 actors. Each migrated record keeps its v2 `actor` and `at` and is stored with
  `sig: null`.
- Every v3 record is derived from v2 records of **one** actor (refused otherwise), each
  authentic under the v2 trust. The migrator writes the map `[[v3 id, [v2 record ids],
  [v2 blob hashes inlined]] …]` as a blob, and one `attest` of the reserved schema `migration@1`,
  signed by the migrator checker (`checker sha256:<D>`, D the digest of the migrator's whole
  import closure: the script, `warrant3/`, `warrant/`), whose `body.target` is the map blob and whose
  body names the v2 snapshot digest, the v2 trust digest and the counts. This record is the
  genesis of the v3 store.
- A `sig: null` record is **authentic** for a receiver iff an authentic `migration@1` attest by
  an actor holding the role `migrator` in its profile lists its id in its map. Its basis is
  reported as `MIGRATED`, never as `SIGNED`.
- The receiver who also holds the v2 store has a second, independent basis: **replay**.
  `tools/migrate_v2_to_v3.py verify` re-runs the migration from the v2 store the genesis names
  and refuses any difference in ids. Signature (custody of the migrator's seed) and replay
  (re-execution) are distinct bases and are reported apart; neither is upgraded into the other.

## 10. Assessment, trust and profile

`assess(snapshot, trust, profile, as_of)` is a pure function. Records dated after `as_of` are
not in the view. The **trust** binds keys to actors (`{"warrant": "trust/3", "keys":
{actor: [public hex…]}}`); it authenticates and authorizes nothing. The **profile** is the
receiver's own: `{"warrant": "profile/3", "roles": {role: [actor…]}}`. Roles decide whose
schemas count, who may succeed, whose attestations meet a guard, whose challenges count, who
replays and who migrates. The same bytes give different verdicts to receivers with different
profiles; nothing in the store can grant a role.

The report, in one pass, holds: the snapshot digest, the assessment id (the digest of the
snapshot digest, the trust, the profile, `as_of` and the code identity), `completeness:
UNATTESTED`, rejected records with reasons, chain forks, schemas, every lineage (versions in
order, leaf, effective version, state, verdict `OK`/`FORK`, excluded successors), every
version's guard verdict (`NONE`/`MET`/`PENDING`/`INVALID`, with who accepted and what is
waited for), every challenge (counted, why, reproductions, standing), and the graph (nodes and
typed edges). **Reliance** on a lineage is `ELIGIBLE` iff it is not forked, its effective version
exists and no counted challenge stands against it; `CHALLENGED`, `PENDING` (nothing effective
yet), `FORK` otherwise.
